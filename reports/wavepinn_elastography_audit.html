WavePINN · SWE Temporal Super-Resolution · Model Audit

The ruler was broken, not the model

A full audit of v10b through v14 for 500 Hz → mid-PRF shear wave elastography. Every viscosity and shear-modulus number published before this audit was an artefact of a 2π unit error in the evaluation code. Re-measured correctly, the model was already far better than reported — and a targeted retrain improved it further.

Date 2026-08-24 Variants v10b · v12 · v13 · v14 · v14c · v14d Hardware 1× NVIDIA L4 23 GB Estimator src/analysis/dispersion.py v1

Bottom line

1. The reported viscosity failure was a measurement bug. estimate_voigt divided rad/s by cycles/m, inflating every phase velocity by 2π. It returned NaN on 63% of ground-truth files and absurd viscosities (20–37 Pa·s) on the rest. On synthetic waves with known answers it returns G′ = 0.000, η = 21.2 for a true 1 kPa / 0.5 Pa·s medium.

2. Measured correctly, v12 was already good: η within 21% and G′ within 10% of matched ground truth, against 46% and 63% for linear interpolation.

3. v14 improves on every matched-GT metric — η error 21%→14%, G′ 10%→8%, cs MAE 0.205→0.140 m/s, and it recovers 39% more of the true high-frequency energy — purely from honest supervision and honest model selection, with no new loss terms.

4. v14d, the follow-up run, gives the best reconstruction of all — highest band coherence (0.778), lowest interior error, best G′ bias (+0%) — and recovers most of v14's cross-acquisition regression. No single checkpoint dominates: v14 is the viscosity model, v14d is the reconstruction and G′ model.

5. One honest caveat: all three models over-estimate viscosity on the cross-acquisition 500 Hz protocol, where ground truth is a different-day acquisition. v12 remains best there. That protocol's confound is why the matched-GT table is the primary one.

01

Where this started

The model does temporal super-resolution of shear-wave velocity movies: 5 input frames at low PRF become 49 dense frames. Shear-wave speed cs was acceptable, but the parameters the work exists for — viscosity η and dynamic shear modulus G′ — looked badly wrong, and adding data (v13) made things worse.

02

Problem 1 — the evaluation code could not measure viscosity at all

Defect

scripts/evaluate_wavepinn_v10.py:estimate_voigt (lines 118–163) computed kxs = np.fft.fftfreq(W, dx), which returns cycles per metre, then formed phase velocity as cs = omega / kx with omega in radians per second. Every phase velocity came out 6.28× too large. That collided with the function's own 0.2 < cs < 12 m/s admissibility gate, so fewer than three frequency bins survived and the routine returned NaN — or fit a Kelvin-Voigt model to three or four wildly inflated points.

The decisive test was synthetic: Kelvin-Voigt waves built in the frequency domain with a known G′ and η, so the correct answer is not in question.

Physical viscosity for these gel phantoms sits near 1–2 Pa·s. A routine that answers 21.2 Pa·s or NaN for every case cannot be used to judge a model.

What this invalidates

Every G′ and η figure in the previous evaluation reports — including the ground-truth columns. The headline that looked like a model failure was two artefacts being compared to each other.

Fix

A rebuilt estimator, src/analysis/dispersion.py, following standard 2D-Fourier SWE dispersion practice: depth-averaging, push localisation, one-directional segments, Tukey windowing, zero-padding, single-quadrant directional filtering, SNR-gated per-frequency wavenumber peaks with thresholded centroid refinement, band selection, and an amplitude-weighted Kelvin-Voigt fit. The old function is left in place, unmodified, so the before/after contrast stays reproducible.

A physical limit we did not know about

Finding

The ROI is 16.2 mm wide and the push usually sits mid-ROI, leaving only a 5–10 mm one-sided aperture. Wavenumber resolution scales as 2π/L, so the resolvable band (≈200–1100 Hz) sits above the elastic regime, where c(ω) → √(2ωη/ρ) becomes independent of G′. Widening the synthetic aperture to 32 mm recovers G′ = 9.0 / η = 3.18 for a true 10.0 / 3.0 medium; at the real aperture G′ degrades to 30% error while η stays at 7%.

Consequence for the paper: η is the well-identified viscoelastic parameter of this geometry; G′ is only weakly identifiable and must be quoted with that caveat, alongside the conventional ρcs² elastic estimate. Model-vs-GT comparisons remain valid because the aperture bias is common-mode when both sides pass through the same estimator and band.

04

Problem 2 — the model was trained and selected against fabricated targets

Defect

For every 500 Hz sample, iq_velocity_dataset_v3.py built the dense target by linearly interpolating the same 5 frames the model receives as input. Forty-four of 49 target frames carried no information above 250 Hz, and training against them actively rewarded piecewise-linear output. Worse, early stopping monitored val_500hz_l1 — L1 against that same interpolation — so checkpoint selection preferred the most interpolation-like model. Roughly 43% of all training targets were fabricated this way.

Defect

PRF augmentation was silently inert. Raw IQ for every 5/10/20 kHz sample is absent in this environment, so _load_augmented_iq always failed and fell back to the native step — with the warning swallowed by DataLoader workers. v12 and v13 never saw the declared step {2,4,5,6,8,10} decimations. Separately, 38 of 154 500 Hz training samples lack raw IQ and were training at dt = 24 ms instead of 2 ms, twelve times outside the deployment regime, invisibly.

Fix — v14

Knot-masked supervision: interpolated target frames are excluded from the loss; only frames where ground truth genuinely exists (indices 0, 12, 24, 36, 48) supervise.
Honest model selection: early stopping switched to val_5khz_l1, whose dense ground truth is real — native step 12, output grid identical to the native grid, frame for frame.
Fail-fast data integrity: strict_iq drops samples whose configured PRF step cannot be honoured instead of silently mis-training them (6 dropped from train, 8 from val), and the never-realisable augmentation steps were removed from the config so it states what actually happens.

The V14 loss is bit-identical to V10's when the mask is absent, so v12's recipe stays exactly reproducible.

05

A protocol that can actually settle the question

Every prior evaluation compared a 500 Hz input against a 5 kHz acquisition recorded on a different day, folding acquisition-to-acquisition variation into every error. The matched-GT protocol removes that confound entirely.

A native 5 kHz acquisition stored at sparse_step 12 gives dtin = 2.4 ms (417 Hz effective input PRF — deployment-like, inside the trained range) and dtout = 0.2 ms, exactly the native grid. The model's 49 output frames land frame-for-frame on the same acquisition's own ground truth. No resampling, no cross-acquisition mismatch.

Baselines run through the identical path: linear and cubic-spline interpolation of the same 5 input velocity frames. Linear interpolation is the specific thing the model must beat — it is both the architecture's zero-effort solution and, for 500 Hz samples, the target v12 was trained against.

Methodological catch

High-frequency power ratio is a misleading metric and we nearly reported it. Piecewise-linear interpolation puts a kink at every knot, and kinks radiate broadband energy — so linear interpolation scores a respectable-looking 0.445 HF power ratio while its band-passed correlation with truth is −0.003, i.e. exactly zero. Band coherence, not power, is what separates reconstruction from smoothing. Both are reported below.

Problem 3 — what the model actually gets wrong

With a working instrument, the remaining deficiency became specific and measurable — and it is not the one the old reports implied.

v12 reconstructs high-frequency phase and structure well (band coherence 0.733 over 250–1500 Hz, against ~0.0 for interpolation) but carries only about 46% of the true high-frequency energy. It is an amplitude deficit, not a structure failure — the conditional-mean shrinkage an L1/L2 objective produces. A control run showed the deficit is both global and frequency-selective:

08

Two negative results, reported in full

v14c — the spectral loss backfired

Failed experiment

A log-magnitude temporal-spectral loss (weight 0.2, frequency-weighted 1→2 across 250–1500 Hz) was added to fix the amplitude deficit. It did the opposite: high-frequency power collapsed to 0.011 of truth, band coherence fell to 0.211, η error hit 139%, and training early-stopped at epoch 77 with val L1 0.0454 against v14's 0.0253.

Cause: log(|P| + 1e-6) is dominated by the near-zero high-frequency bins, where a tiny absolute difference becomes an enormous log difference. That term's gradient swamped the pixel losses and drove the model to a degenerate low-energy solution. If a spectral term is retried it must be renormalised per sample, or compare band-integrated energies rather than per-bin magnitudes.

This protocol's ground truth is a different-day acquisition of the same plane, so it carries a confound the matched-GT protocol does not — but all three models face it equally, so the relative ordering is real. v12 is the best model here, and all three over-estimate viscosity, with predicted spread 2–4× wider than the ground-truth spread. That pattern suggests 500 Hz → 6 kHz dispersion is intrinsically unstable, not that one model is broken.

Diagnosis, and how far the fix got

Zeroing the interpolated frames left the model completely unconstrained between knots in the 500 Hz regime — the one regime that matters most for deployment. The band table in §07 shows the consequence: v14 recovers far more energy, but with a distorted shape — a trough at 250–500 Hz followed by a bump above it. That non-monotonic spectrum inflates the fitted dispersion slope, and hence η.

v14d weighted interpolated frames at 0.2 rather than 0, on the physical argument that below the 250 Hz knot Nyquist linear interpolation is a fair estimate of the envelope. It worked partially: η median error fell from 65% to 44%, bias from +55% to +29%, and the correlation with ground truth was restored from −0.09 to +0.39. But it did not reach v12's 35% / +20% / +0.43, and cs MAE worsened to 1.028 m/s. The cross-acquisition regression is reduced, not eliminated.

The mid-PRF deliverable

500 Hz in, 2–3 kHz out. Mid-PRF outputs are exact integer decimations of the dense field — 6 kHz ÷ 3 = 2 kHz, ÷ 2 = 3 kHz — so no interpolation is introduced and one checkpoint serves every rate.

Reference: cs 2.808 m/s, η 1.254 Pa·s. The decimated field is literally the full-rate field subsampled, so this is a change in what the analysis can see, not in the reconstruction — the estimator's upper frequency limit tracks the output Nyquist.

Honest trade-off

Mid-PRF output is not free. Time-of-arrival cs degrades sharply with coarser output sampling (0.205 → 0.907 → 1.576 m/s) because lag resolution depends directly on frame spacing. Viscosity is far more tolerant, staying between 0.78 and 1.10 Pa·s MAE across all three rates. For the paper, the defensible claim is that 2–3 kHz output is sufficient for viscoelastic characterisation but not for the sharpest shear-speed estimates. If cs precision matters, ship the full-rate field.

11

Where this leaves the paper

Defensible claims today

  • The model reconstructs genuine high-frequency shear-wave content that temporal interpolation cannot: band coherence 0.778 over 250–1500 Hz against −0.003 for linear interpolation. This is the strongest single result in the study.
  • On matched ground truth, viscosity is recovered to 14% median error (v14) and dynamic shear modulus to 8–10% with essentially zero bias, against 46% and 63% for the interpolation baseline.
  • Shear wave speed MAE is 0.140 m/s (v14), a tenfold improvement on interpolation (1.394 m/s).
  • Ground-truth repeatability of 0.23% (η) and 0.37% (G′) establishes that these differences sit far above measurement noise.
  • Mid-PRF (2–3 kHz) output preserves viscoelastic accuracy; the cs trade-off is quantified rather than hidden.

Open items

  1. The cross-acquisition η regression is reduced but unresolved. v14d recovered most of it (65%→44%, correlation restored to +0.39) without reaching v12's 35%. The next step is frequency-selective supervision — constrain interpolated frames only below the 250 Hz knot Nyquist, where linear interpolation is actually valid, instead of applying a flat scalar weight across all frequencies.
  2. G′ is aperture-limited. Report it with the ρcs² elastic estimate alongside and state the limitation — a wider ROI or an edge-positioned push would fix it at acquisition time, not in software.
  3. 1FT ground truth is unreliable (within-recording η CV 76%). Exclude it from headline tables or flag it. Note it is 18 of the 36 matched-GT acquisitions, so it carries real weight in those aggregates.
  4. Split contamination remains. The frozen test split has 144 high-PRF same-plane siblings in train. Re-carving plane-disjoint across all PRFs is outstanding and needed before publication.
  5. Goat-heart and PVA checks have been re-pointed at the new estimator but not yet re-run.
Recommendation

Ship v14d as the primary checkpoint — it has the best waveform fidelity, the best G′, the best training monitor, and it repairs most of v14's regression. Quote η from v14 where viscosity is the headline quantity, and say so.

Make the matched-GT protocol the paper's primary quantitative table, with the cross-acquisition split as a supplementary check whose different-day confound is stated explicitly. All pre-audit G′/η figures must be withdrawn, not revised — including the ground-truth columns.
